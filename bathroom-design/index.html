For your builder

Set-out and trade notes

The 1000 mm tile line

Start the green with a full tile at 1000 AFFL on every wall so the line reads cleanly, and cut at the ceiling. On the lower walls, lay a full 600 tile below the line and take the cut at the floor. A slim brushed brass trim at the change is optional.

Sheets and grout lines

The olive tile comes on 300 × 300 sheets, and the Slate Grey grout will show every joint as a grey line, so the sheet joints must disappear into the tile grid. Start full sheets at the 1000 line, keep lines plumb and blend sheets from several boxes. The stone beige uses a warm Mud Berry grout that almost disappears.

Walk-in shower falls

With no door or hob, the shower floor falls to its square tile insert waste and the main floor falls to its own. A square waste needs the floor to fall from all four sides, so 600 × 600 tiles need diagonal envelope cuts around it. Agree the cut layout with the tiler before ordering. The glass stays 600 wide to keep the entry usable, so expect some splash at the opening; the floor waste on the dry side catches it. Waterproofing to AS 3740 by a licensed waterproofer.

Tiled ledge

The ledge top and front are clad in the floor tile. Mitre the front edge so no cut edge shows, and line up the ledge-front joints with the floor joints below.

Electrical

New circuits for the heated poles on a timer and the cabinet LED driver. Removing the shower door changes the wet-area zones under AS/NZS 3000, so the electrician should set outlet positions.

Strata

Bathroom works in a strata building usually need owners corporation approval, especially where waterproofing, plumbing or walls change. Check your by-laws before booking trades.

Open items

To confirm before ordering

  • Olive tile size. The quote lists 50 × 150 tiles on 300 × 300 sheets, so the niche is 600 × 300 (two sheets) with its sill on the row line at 1300. The olive photo you sent earlier looked longer than 50 × 150, so check with Beaumont that it's the same tile.
  • Niche wall construction. The door-side wall needs a stud cavity with no pipes or cables at the niche position. If it's masonry or a common wall, the niche needs strata sign-off or a new location.
  • Glass fixing with clamps only. Ask the glazier to confirm a 600 panel is rigid on wall and floor clamps alone, and whether the floor clamps need to be set before or after the tiles go down. If it needs more support, a brass clamp to the ceiling is less visible than a bar across the entry.
  • Cistern and pan. The quoted suite is a floor-standing back-to-wall pan with a concealed cistern, not a wall-hung pan. Check the cistern fits inside the 1000 high, 200 deep ledge with access through the flush button, and that the pan's waste lines up with the existing floor outlet.
  • Basin and tap. The quoted 590 basin has one tap hole on its rear deck, so the mixer mounts on the basin. Check the mixer spout reaches the middle of the bowl, and confirm basin and pan heights before rough-in.
  • Cabinet size and depth. 1600 × 750 is the proposal. Confirm the LED strip is concealed on all four sides and the driver has an access point.
  • Door stop on the door. Rainco lists the Lombardy as a floor stop. Check it can be screwed to the door face, or pick their door-mounted version, and set it so it meets the right-hand wall before the handle does.
  • Towel pole height and spacing. Drawn 1200 tall, 300 apart. Check they clear the door swing and the door handle.
